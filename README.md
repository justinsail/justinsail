# Justin Bradshaw

My career started on stage and ended up in tech, as a Digital Life Coach (emphasis on the digital life part). I moved from the creative, emotion-based world of acting and landed in the analytical, evidence-based world of zeroes and ones. Even though they are worlds apart on the surface, I realized I was kinda doing what I'd been doing on stage: translating for groups that don't speak the same language. The actors understood emotion but not systems. The engineers understood systems but not people. I kept finding myself in the middle, straddling both sides of the brain and being tugged toward tech. Now the fault line runs between humans and AI, and I'm standing right there on it again, deep in the valley between them, sharing what I find.

## What I do now

I run [MacCog](https://maccog.com), a solo Apple Consultancy out of San Luis Obispo, California. My clients are a diverse mix of small businesses and families who run their lives and work on Apple hardware. I've been doing this since before the iPhone existed. I know where the bodies are buried in macOS, and I love this stuff.

It's not easy to describe the role I play in my client's lives but it's like a *Digital Life* Coach. Not a life coach who does Zoom calls... but a coach for your entire Digital Life! I go deep into the Apple ecosystem and wide into tech that extends from it. Clients keep me on retainer to make their tech lives work.

Lately the fastest-growing part is helping them bring the magic of AI into their files, calendars, and businesses... leading to a new nickname: the AI Mac Guy. In January 2026 when Claude Code got really good, I built my own second brain on top of Claude, Obsidian, and a collection of skills I've built to run it all. I use it every day to run my business, build software and keep myself organized. My AI Coaching clients become members of a program I call the Digital Life Coach Operating System, or dlcOS: I install a version of that private and secure AI system on their Macs and teach them to run it. It's not a product as much as an extension of the job I've always had: figure out what works and then help other humans build it for their own digital life.

I'm also available for speaking, and unlike most tech guys, I'm experienced on stage. If you're putting together an event about AI, human creativity, or what the next ten years look like for people who aren't engineers, we might be a fit.
